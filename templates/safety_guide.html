Heatwave Safety & First Aid Guide

Essential guidance for families, seniors, and community volunteers during peak summer months.

1. Heat Exhaustion (Warning Phase)

Occurs due to heavy sweating without adequate water/electrolyte replacement.

  • Heavy sweating, cold pale clammy skin
  • Rapid, weak pulse and dizziness
  • Nausea, muscle cramps, and extreme fatigue
  • Headache and dark urine
Action: Move to shade, loosen clothing, sip salted water or ORS slowly.

2. Heatstroke (Medical Emergency)

Body thermoregulation fails; core temperature reaches >40°C.

  • High body temperature (>103°F / 39.5°C)
  • Hot, red, dry skin (sweating has halted)
  • Confusion, slurred speech, or delirium
  • Loss of consciousness or convulsions
Immediate Action: Call 108 ambulance, press SOS, soak victim in cold water.

Safe Hours & Daily Routine

Time Window Solar Exposure Recommended Activity for High-Risk Groups
05:30 AM – 09:30 AM Low Safest window for market shopping, outdoor chores, manual labour, and ventilation of home.
10:00 AM – 04:30 PM Extreme Danger Stay indoors. Avoid direct sun. Tin-roof residents should move to neighborhood shelters or shaded verandas.
05:00 PM – 08:00 PM Moderate Heat Trap Hydrate with electrolytes. Check on elderly neighbors living alone.

Evidence-Based Home Cooling Tips

  • Tin & Asbestos Roof Mitigation: Cover the top of tin roofs with dry straw, burlap sacks, or painted white lime wash to reflect 70% of radiant solar heat.
  • Wet Cotton Drape: Place a damp cotton towel around the back of the neck and on pulse points (wrists, temples) to speed up evaporative cooling.
  • Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS): Stir 1 packet of standard WHO-formula ORS into 1 litre of clean drinking water. Alternatively: 1 litre water + 6 teaspoons sugar + 1/2 teaspoon salt.
  • Avoid Caffeinated & Sugary Drinks: Alcohol, tea, and sweetened carbonated drinks increase kidney fluid loss through diuresis.
